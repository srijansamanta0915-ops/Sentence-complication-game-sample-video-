Developmental Language Disorder or DLD is besically a condition that changes how a person can actually understand or use language. Developmental Language Disorder can make talking, learning and daily tasks harder. The challenges of Developmental Language Disorder might stay as the child grows.

For example I have seen a child, with Developmental Language Disorder who knows what they want to say but cannot find the words. Developmental Language Disorder can make it harder for them to follow an instruction answer a question explain a story or remember what they have heard.

DLD can affect two areas of language:

* language: Understanding what other people say.

* Expressive language: Using words and sentences to communicate thoughts and ideas.

Some children experience difficulties in both areas while others have difficulty with one than the other.

### Common Difficulties Associated with DLD

The signs of DLD can vary from one child to another. Some children may have a vocabulary than expected speak in sentences or take longer to express their thoughts.  It may  difficult to understand instructions or participate in conversations for others .

One common difficulty is. Remembering vocabulary. A child may understand a word but struggle with a familiar one. For example they may know the word ". Have difficulty understanding or using a word such as "enormous.”

Grammar and sentence structure can also be challenging. Words such as "before " "after," "because," and "unless" may be harder to understand when they appear in sentences.

For instance a child may easily understand:

> "The girl is reading a book.”

However the following sentence may be more difficult:

> "Before the girl went outside she finished reading her book.”

This can make classroom lessons and instructions harder to follow.

Another area that may be affected is processing. This refers to the ability to understand spoken language and respond to it. A child may need time to understand a question recognize a word or decide what to say. They may also ask others to repeat themselves.

DLD can also affect working memory, which's the ability to temporarily remember spoken information and use it. For example a child may forget part of a sentence. Remember some of a multi-step instruction.

If a teacher says:

> "Take out your notebook write the date and underline the heading.”

a child with DLD might remember part of the instruction. Giving the directions one step at a time may make them easier to follow.

Language difficulties can affect schoolwork well. Children with DLD may find reading comprehension, writing, spelling and learning vocabulary more difficult. Sometimes they may understand a topic but struggle to explain their answer

Communication difficulties can also influence interaction. A child may find it hard to join a conversation understand jokes explain feelings or keep up when people speak quickly. These difficulties do not mean that the child is not intelligent or does not want to communicate.

The severity of DLD are from person to person. Some individuals have their difficulties while others might need support at school and in daily life. Language skills can improve with practice and appropriate support also some difficulties may continue into adulthood.

So DLD is not only about making language mistakes. It is a language difficulty which can affect functioning. Only a speech-language therapist or speech-language pathologist can assess these difficulties. Recommend support.

Support may includes speech and language therapy, visual aids, repetition, shorter instructions and activities that help children practise vocabulary, sentence formation and understanding the spoken language.

∆:Figuring Out

The purpose of this project is to create a language-based games that allow us to observe different language skills. The activities are designed for awareness purposes not for diagnosing DLD.

∆ 1:Word–Picture Matching Game

In this game a picture appears on the screen along with a few word options. The player has to select the word that matches the picture.

For example the screen may show a picture of an apple with the options Apple, Chair and Book.

The game can begin with objects. As the player progresses it can introduce vocabulary or pictures that look similar making the activity more challenging.

∆ Measures:

* accuracy

* Response time

* Number of answers

* Improvement after repeated practice

This activity can help us observe how easily a player connects words with their meanings. However performance may also depend on attention, familiarity with the pictures and reading ability.

∆2: Sentence Completion Game

In this activity the player sees a sentence with one missing word. Chooses the option that completes it correctly.

∆ Example:

> The boy is drinking ___.
[Options]: (a)Water (b) Running (c)Blue

Now the sentences  become more difficult as the player progresses. Later rounds include sentences or words that require an understanding of grammar.

∆ Measurement:

(A) Sentence comprehension

(B)Grammar accuracy 

(C)Response time

(D)Number of mistakes

∆:This game  helps us  how well a player observes and understands the meaning of a sentence and selects a word.

∆3:Listening and Instruction Game

In this game the player listens to an instruction. Follows it on the screen.

For example:

> "Click the circle click the blue square.”

The game can begin with one-step instructions. Gradually move to two to  three-step instructions.

∆Measurement:

(A)Accuracy in the following instructions.

(B)Number of completed steps

(C)Response time

(D) Mistakes in the order of actions

So this activity  help us observe how well a player understands and remembers the instructions.

∆4: Word-Order Sentence Game

In this activity the words of a sentence are displayed in the order. The player has to arrange them to form a sentence.

For example:

> eating / is / The / boy / an apple

answer:

> The boy is eating an apple.

The difficulty can be increased by adding sentences, words or connecting words such as "because" and "although.”

Measures:

* Sentence-order accuracy

* Time taken to arrange the words

* Number of attempts

* Improvement with practice

This activity can help us observe how well a player recognizes sentence structure and grammar.

∆5: Verbal Memory Sequence Game

In this game a short sequence of words is displayed on the screen. Played through audio. After a delay the player has to remember and arrange the words in the order.

For example:

> Cat → Tree → Ball

The player must. Arrange the words correctly. The sequence can become longer in each round.

∆Measurement:

(A) Number of recalled words

(B)Sequence accuracy

(C)Response time

(D)Improvement across rounds

This activity help us observe memory and attention. However performance may also be influenced by concentration, familiarity with the words and the player’s experience with the game.

###::

DLD can affect aspects of language including vocabulary grammar, understanding spoken instructions and verbal memory. These difficulties may influence a child’s learning, communication and social interaction.

The proposed games provide a way to explore these language skills through activities. By recording accuracy response time and improvement the project can demonstrate how different language tasks work and how performance may change with practice.

However the games are not tools. A proper assessment of DLD requires evaluation by a speech-language professional. The main purpose of this project is to promote awareness and provide a way to understand language-related difficulties.
